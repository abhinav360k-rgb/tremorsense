TremorSense Open the app

A tremor, measured with the phone in your pocket

TremorSense uses a phone's motion sensor to measure how fast and how strongly a hand shakes, then turns repeated tests into a report a patient can bring to their doctor.

Open the app Free. Runs in the phone's browser on iPhone and Android, with nothing to download.

Why I built it

Tremors from conditions like Parkinson's disease and essential tremor change from hour to hour. They can get better or worse as medication wears off, with stress, or with tiredness. But a neurologist usually sees a patient for a short visit every few months and judges the tremor by eye.

Almost every phone already has an accelerometer that measures motion many times a second. TremorSense uses it to give patients a way to measure their tremor at home, as often as they like, and show their doctor numbers instead of describing it from memory.

How a test works

  1. Record

    The phone rests on the back of the hand, or on an outstretched palm. For 11 seconds, the accelerometer reports movement in three directions (X, Y and Z) about 60 to 100 times a second.

  2. Line up the readings

    Phones don't report at perfectly even times, so the readings are placed onto an even grid of 50 per second using smooth curves between points.

  3. Remove slow movement

    A high-pass filter removes anything slower than one shake per second, like gravity and the arm slowly drifting. What's left is the fast shaking.

  4. Find the rhythm

    A Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) breaks the movement into how much shaking happens at each speed. The tallest peak between 3 and 12 shakes per second (Hz) is the tremor.

  5. Save and report

    The speed and strength are saved on the phone. Over days and weeks, the tests add up to a printable report for the doctor.

How I tested it

To trust the numbers, I fed in fake tremors where I already knew the right answer and checked that TremorSense found it. These tests are running live in your browser right now.

Two problems the tests caught

A fake peak at double the speed. My first plan combined the three directions into one number before analyzing. Because that number is never negative, a 5 Hz tremor showed up as 10 Hz, and an 8 Hz tremor disappeared completely because 16 Hz is outside the search range. Tick the box in "Try the math" to see it happen. TremorSense now analyzes each direction separately and combines the results afterward.

Fast tremors measured too weak. At 12 Hz, the strength came out about 20% too low. Connecting readings with straight lines was flattening the fastest wiggles, so I switched to smooth curves and used finer frequency steps. Now every test is within 10%.

What it can't do

  • It doesn't diagnose anything. Different conditions can cause tremors at similar speeds. TremorSense measures, and a doctor decides what it means.
  • Phones differ. Sensors vary between models, so results are best compared on the same phone.
  • It hasn't been clinically tested. It's been checked against known signals, not against medical equipment.
  • It's private. There are no accounts and no server. Results never leave the phone.
